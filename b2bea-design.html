01 / Audit

What's actually broken about the current site.

The existing site works as an association brochure. It does not work as the front door to a professional network. Below are the structural problems — not pixel critiques — that block the positioning the team wants.

The site reads as

An industry association with classes, badges, and a vendor wall.

Hero promises "Advancing the future of B2B eCommerce" — boardroom-vague. The first three CTAs (Join Now, Learn More, Join Today) compete for attention without segmenting the visitor. The page rewards no one quickly: executives, practitioners, and vendors all see the same wall.

It needs to read as

A professional network built for the people doing the work.

PMA — the reference — leads with social proof ("300k+ community members"), segments by career level in the first scroll, and uses editorial voice to feel like a publication, not a portal. The job is the same here: show the visitor who's inside, prove the network has gravity, and route them to their lane fast.

Severity · High · Hero

The hero says nothing memorable.

"Advancing the Future of B2B eCommerce Through Education and Connection" is the kind of sentence every association in the world could put on its homepage. It's worth zero search intent and zero recall.

→ Lead with a specific, opinionated value claim — who it's for and what they get. Stat trio in-hero.
Severity · High · Routing

No segmentation in the first scroll.

A manufacturer, a software vendor, and a consultant all need different things from this site, and the homepage treats them identically. The reference does this in the first viewport with a "tell us who you are" card grid.

→ Persona selector with 5 lanes (Manufacturer · Distributor · Software · Agency · Consultant) right under the hero.
Severity · High · CTA conflict

Three "Join" CTAs in the first 600 pixels.

"Join Now" (top nav), "Join Now" (hero), and "Learn More" (certificate block) compete before the visitor knows why they'd join. Add the orange ticket bar at the very top and there are four orange clickables stacked.

→ One primary action per viewport. Top-of-page bar should be a soft signal, not a CTA.
Severity · Medium · Voice

Generic, association-tone copy throughout.

"Discover & Make Connections in B2B eCommerce," "Find a B2B ecommerce expert," "Become a Member Today" — every headline could belong to any trade body. No point of view, no italic, no character.

→ Editorial voice with display serif + italic accents on the differentiator words.
Severity · Medium · Visual rhythm

One palette, many sections, no contrast.

Navy → white → navy → white → navy. The page has eight major bands and uses essentially two background colors. Compare to PMA's pastel rotation (peach, mint, cream, navy) that signals each section is a different room.

→ Add warm tonal surfaces (peach, cream, mint) as section separators. Reserve navy for emphasis.
Severity · Medium · Cards

News cards look identical and earn no attention.

The "Latest News & Insights" grid uses the same card shell with cropped photos for every item. There's no format differentiation (guide vs. report vs. interview) and no signal of why a given piece matters.

→ Card variants by format. Tonal media areas. Author attribution prominent. Estimated read time.
Severity · Medium · Vendor wall

The vendor wall is the longest section on the page.

Roughly 40+ vendor logos in a flat grid sit between membership CTAs. For a site positioned as a practitioner network, this much vendor real estate signals the inverse — vendors first, practitioners second.

→ Compress to a 6-logo trust strip. Move the full vendor directory to its own "Exchange" page.
Severity · Medium · Social proof

No people on the page who feel like members.

One YouTube thumbnail of a man with a microphone. One audience photo, taken from behind. No member faces, no quotes, no titles. A "LinkedIn for B2B eCommerce" should put member identities in the first two scrolls.

→ Practitioner spotlight + member directory teaser with at least 4 named profiles above the fold-2.
Severity · Low · IA

Top nav has too many parallel categories.

Membership, Academy, Events, Resources, News & Insights, About — six parallel menus, several of which overlap ("Resources" vs "News & Insights"). The mental model is shallow and wide, not deep and clear.

→ Collapse to 4 primary: Insights · Events · Careers · Academy. Membership lives in the CTA.
Severity · Low · Typography

One sans-serif for everything.

Display, headings, body, captions — all the same family at different weights. No editorial contrast, which flattens the page and removes a major lever for brand voice.

→ Pair a characterful display serif (Fraunces) with a clean UI sans (General Sans).

Type · Fraunces (display) + General Sans (UI)

Fraunces brings the editorial italic the brand needs. General Sans handles dense UI with character but without Inter's ubiquity. Use italic Fraunces only for emphasis words, never whole sentences.
